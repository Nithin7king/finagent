PERSONAL BUDGETING FRAMEWORKS & GUIDELINES

=== 1. THE 50/30/20 RULE ===

Popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in "All Your Worth" (2005).

How it works — split after-tax income into:
- 50% Needs: rent/EMI, groceries, utilities, transportation, minimum debt payments, insurance
- 30% Wants: dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping, travel, hobbies
- 20% Savings & Debt Repayment: emergency fund, retirement (EPF/PPF/NPS), investments, extra debt payments

Example (₹60,000/month take-home):
- Needs: ₹30,000 — rent ₹15,000 + groceries ₹5,000 + transport ₹3,000 + utilities ₹4,000 + insurance ₹3,000
- Wants: ₹18,000 — Zomato ₹3,000 + subscriptions ₹2,000 + shopping ₹8,000 + entertainment ₹5,000
- Savings: ₹12,000 — emergency fund ₹5,000 + SIP ₹5,000 + extra loan payment ₹2,000

When to use: Best for beginners who want a simple, flexible budget.
Limitation: "Needs" vs "Wants" distinction can be subjective.

=== 2. ZERO-BASED BUDGETING (ZBB) ===

Every rupee of income is assigned a purpose. Income minus all allocations = ₹0.

Steps:
1. List your monthly income (all sources)
2. List all expenses (fixed + variable)
3. Assign every rupee to a category
4. If you have money left → allocate to savings or debt
5. If you're in deficit → reduce discretionary categories

Best for: People who want strict control over spending, debt repayment mode.
Tool: YNAB (You Need A Budget), or a simple spreadsheet.

=== 3. THE ENVELOPE METHOD ===

Physical (or digital) envelopes for each spending category. When the envelope is empty, stop spending.

Digital equivalents:
- Separate savings accounts for each goal
- Apps that segment your budget by category

=== 4. PAY YOURSELF FIRST ===

On the day your salary arrives, immediately transfer the savings amount to a separate account.
Only spend from what remains.

Recommended savings rate by life stage:
- 20s (career start): 20–30% of income
- 30s (family/home): 15–25%
- 40s (peak earning): 25–35%
- 50s+ (pre-retirement): 30–40%

=== 5. DEBT AVALANCHE vs SNOWBALL ===

Debt Avalanche (mathematically optimal):
- Pay minimum on all debts
- Put extra money toward the HIGHEST interest rate debt first
- Minimizes total interest paid

Debt Snowball (psychologically motivating):
- Pay minimum on all debts
- Put extra money toward the SMALLEST balance first
- Builds momentum through quick wins

=== SAVINGS BENCHMARKS (INDIA) ===

Emergency Fund:
- Target: 3–6 months of monthly expenses
- 3 months if you have stable government/PSU job
- 6 months if self-employed or in volatile sector
- Where to keep: high-yield savings account (SBI Savings = 2.7%, HDFC = 3%), liquid funds (5–6.5% returns), or short-duration debt funds

Retirement Savings:
- Rule of thumb: save 15% of gross income for retirement from age 25
- NPS: national pension system, employer can contribute, tax benefits under 80CCD
- EPF: 12% of basic salary mandatory, employer matches 12% (8.33% to EPS, 3.67% to EPF)
- PPF: voluntary, ₹500 min – ₹1.5L max per year, 15-year lock-in, tax-free returns

Home Purchase Guideline:
- EMI should not exceed 40% of monthly take-home pay
- Down payment: ideally 20–25% of property value
- Include stamp duty (4–7% in most states) + registration costs

=== SUBSCRIPTION MANAGEMENT ===

"Subscription creep" — the gradual accumulation of small recurring charges.

Monthly subscription audit checklist:
1. List ALL recurring charges (streaming, apps, gym, cloud storage, etc.)
2. Identify which you used in the last 30 days
3. Cancel any unused for > 2 months
4. Negotiate annual plans (typically 15–30% cheaper than monthly)

Rule of thumb: Total subscriptions should not exceed 5% of monthly income.
