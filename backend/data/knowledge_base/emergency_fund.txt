EMERGENCY FUND GUIDELINES

=== WHAT IS AN EMERGENCY FUND? ===

An emergency fund is money set aside specifically for unexpected financial emergencies:
- Job loss or sudden income disruption
- Medical emergency (hospitalization, surgery)
- Urgent vehicle or home repair
- Family emergency requiring travel
- Sudden large expense not in budget

It is NOT for: planned expenses, vacations, shopping sales, or investment opportunities.

=== HOW MUCH SHOULD YOU SAVE? ===

The 3–6 Month Rule:
- Calculate your MONTHLY ESSENTIAL EXPENSES (not income):
  - Rent/EMI
  - Groceries and food
  - Utilities (electricity, internet, mobile)
  - Transport to work
  - Insurance premiums (health, term)
  - Minimum loan payments
  - Any essential medication

Example: If monthly essentials = ₹30,000
  → 3-month fund = ₹90,000
  → 6-month fund = ₹1,80,000

Who needs 3 months:
- Stable government / PSU job with pension
- Dual-income household with stable jobs
- Minimal debt, low dependents

Who needs 6 months:
- Private sector job with layoff risk
- Self-employed / freelancer / business owner
- Single income household
- High fixed debt obligations (EMIs)
- Health conditions or elderly dependents

Financial advisors often recommend 12 months for entrepreneurs.

=== WHERE TO KEEP YOUR EMERGENCY FUND ===

Criteria: ACCESSIBLE (within 1–2 days), SAFE (no market risk), LOW BUT POSITIVE RETURN

Option 1 — High-Yield Savings Account:
- SBI: 2.70% p.a.
- HDFC: 3.00% p.a.
- Kotak 811: 3.50% p.a.
- Pros: Instant access, fully liquid
- Cons: Returns often below inflation

Option 2 — Liquid Mutual Fund:
- Parag Parikh Liquid Fund, HDFC Liquid Fund, etc.
- Returns: ~6.5–7.5% p.a. (money market instruments)
- Redemption: T+1 business day (instant redemption up to ₹50,000 available)
- Low risk (no credit risk for top-rated liquid funds)
- Best balance of safety and returns

Option 3 — Sweep-in FD (Auto-sweep):
- Linked to savings account
- Excess balance auto-converted to FD at higher rates (6.5–7%)
- Auto-broken when you withdraw — acts like a liquid account

Option 4 — Short-Duration Debt Fund:
- Slightly higher returns than liquid fund (7–8%)
- Redemption: T+1 to T+2
- Suitable for amounts you won't need for 1–3 months

DO NOT keep emergency fund in:
- Stock market (could be down 30% when you need it most)
- Locked FDs without premature withdrawal option
- Illiquid assets (gold jewelry, real estate)

=== HOW TO BUILD YOUR EMERGENCY FUND ===

Step 1 — Start with ₹5,000–₹10,000 "starter emergency fund" while paying off debt.
Step 2 — Clear all high-interest debt (credit cards).
Step 3 — Build to 1-month expenses.
Step 4 — Build to 3-month fund.
Step 5 — Build to 6-month fund while also investing for growth.

Monthly contribution needed:
- To build ₹1,50,000 in 12 months: ₹12,500/month
- To build ₹1,50,000 in 18 months: ₹8,333/month
- To build ₹1,50,000 in 24 months: ₹6,250/month

=== MAINTAINING YOUR EMERGENCY FUND ===

- After using: Replenish within 6–12 months
- Review annually: Update target as expenses change (rent increase, new EMI, baby)
- Don't merge with other goals: Keep it in a separate account mentally "locked"
- Resist the temptation to invest it when markets are up

=== CREDIT CARD AS EMERGENCY BUFFER? ===

A credit card can supplement but NOT replace an emergency fund:
- Credit card debt = 30–48% annual interest if not paid in full
- Bank can reduce your limit precisely when you need it most
- Job loss may make you unable to make minimum payments
- Emergency fund gives you negotiating power; debt does not
