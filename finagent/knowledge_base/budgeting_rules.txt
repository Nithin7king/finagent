The 50/30/20 Budgeting Rule
This popular budgeting framework suggests dividing your after-tax income into three
buckets: 50% for needs (rent, groceries, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments),
30% for wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping), and 20% for savings
and debt repayment beyond the minimum. It is a simple starting point for people who do
not want to track every single expense category in detail.

Building an Emergency Fund
Financial advisors generally recommend keeping 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses
in a liquid, easily accessible savings account. This fund is meant to cover job loss,
medical emergencies, or major unexpected expenses without requiring high-interest debt.

Reducing Subscription Creep
Subscription creep happens when recurring charges for services you rarely use quietly
accumulate over time. A good practice is to review all recurring charges every three
months and cancel anything that has not been actively used in the last 60 days.

Debt Repayment Strategies
The "avalanche method" prioritizes paying off debt with the highest interest rate first
while making minimum payments on the rest, which minimizes total interest paid over time.
The "snowball method" instead prioritizes paying off the smallest balance first to build
psychological momentum, even though it may cost slightly more interest overall.
